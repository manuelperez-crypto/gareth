01 Why companies use AI

Six business pressures driving adoption.

01
Cost Reduction & Efficiency

AI automates repetitive knowledge work — drafting, summarizing, data extraction, classification — that previously required teams of analysts. McKinsey estimates up to 30% reduction in operational costs for early adopters in financial services.

Back Office Automation Labor Cost
02
Speed of Decision-Making

LLMs compress analysis cycles from days to minutes. Investment memos, due diligence summaries, regulatory filings — tasks that took associate teams a week can be produced in hours, giving firms a material competitive edge on deal speed.

Finance Research M&A
03
Competitive Pressure

When peers adopt AI and reduce costs or cycle times, firms that don't fall behind. Adoption is no longer optional in most industries — it's a baseline requirement to remain price-competitive and retain top talent who expect modern tooling.

Strategy Retention Market Position
04
Customer Experience

AI-powered assistants handle first-line client inquiries, route tickets, and personalize communication at scale — reducing response times from hours to seconds while maintaining the tone and brand voice specific to each firm.

CX Support Personalization
05
Regulatory & Compliance Leverage

AI systems can continuously monitor transactions, flag anomalies, and draft compliance narratives — reducing the manual burden of regulatory reporting across SEC filings, AML checks, and audit trail documentation in financial firms.

SEC AML Audit
06
Data Intelligence at Scale

Organizations sit on vast unstructured data — contracts, emails, call transcripts, filings. AI unlocks this data by extracting patterns, summarizing positions, and identifying risk signals that would be invisible to a human analyst reviewing millions of documents.

05 Why Claude leads in regulated industries

The reasons firms choose Anthropic when the stakes are high.

  • i
    Constitutional AI — built-in safety

    Anthropic developed Constitutional AI (CAI), a training methodology that embeds a set of principles directly into the model. In practice this means Claude is less likely to fabricate citations, make up numbers, or produce confident-sounding but wrong output — a critical property for financial reports and legal documents where an error costs real money.

  • ii
    Longest reliable context window

    Claude processes up to 200,000 tokens — roughly 150,000 words — in a single prompt without degradation. Competitors with similar limits often "forget" information in the middle of the document. For a 300-page offering memorandum or a 200-exhibit due diligence room, this is a hard competitive requirement.

  • iii
    Data privacy by default

    Anthropic's enterprise agreements guarantee that customer data is not used to train future models. For asset managers, law firms, and hospitals handling confidential client information or MNPI (material non-public information), this is a regulatory and contractual necessity, not a preference.

  • iv
    Calibrated uncertainty

    Claude is trained to express when it doesn't know something rather than filling gaps with plausible-sounding errors. In compliance and legal contexts, a model that says "I'm not certain — please verify this against the primary source" is far more valuable than one that confidently states an incorrect regulation or statute.

  • v
    Instruction-following precision

    Financial and legal workflows require structured, consistent output — tables formatted a specific way, sections labeled exactly as instructed, calculations that follow a defined logic. Claude consistently outperforms other models on instruction-following benchmarks, which translates directly into fewer human-correction loops in production workflows.
